A year of being there.

From the Executive Director, to every donor, volunteer, and family who made this year possible.

From
Executive Director
Year
2025

Dear friend,

It has been twelve years since the first patient signed onto our service. He died at home on a Sunday, in his own bed, with a granddaughter reading to him. I tell you about him because every patient since has been some version of him, or her, or theirs — somebody's particular person, in their particular bed, in their particular last hours. That is the work. It does not get larger or smaller. It only multiplies.

This year we walked alongside 1,247 patients. We made 38,400 visits. We were on call 8,760 hours. We answered the phone at 2 AM 1,830 times. We sat at the bedside, in the final hours, for 312 vigils when family could not be there.

Not all of this work was reimbursed. We provided $417,000 of unreimbursed charity care — care for patients with no insurance, gaps in coverage, or circumstances we did not look up. We held a community memorial service twice. We trained 34 new volunteers. We gave 1,109 grieving family members thirteen months of bereavement support, six months past what the regulation requires, because grief does not turn off at month seven.

What your gift made possible

Hospice is largely paid for by Medicare. But the things we are proudest of are not. Volunteer training is not. The bereavement program past month seven is not. The vigil program is not. Music therapy is not. The Spanish-language family materials are not. The respite weekend at our inpatient unit, when a daughter has been awake for forty hours and cannot stand up, is not. Charity care is not.

Your gift this year directly funded those things. To put a number on it: the philanthropic dollars raised cover almost exactly what Medicare does not. Without you, the work that distinguishes Azalea from any other hospice would not exist. With you, it does.

"My mother was on hospice for six days. I had been a caregiver for two years. The first night a nurse came, I slept for nine hours for the first time in I don't know how long. That nurse was your gift to me." — A bereaved daughter, November 2025

What we are working toward in 2026

Three things. First, a dedicated pediatric hospice program — perinatal and pediatric care, in partnership with the children's hospital. We see fewer than a dozen pediatric patients a year, but each one deserves a team trained for them. Second, a second inpatient unit in our southern service area, so no family drives more than 45 minutes to a loved one in symptom crisis. Third, an expansion of community grief support — open groups, free of charge, regardless of whether the loss was on our service.

I will write to you when each of these is built. They will be built because of you.

One last thing

If you have ever been the family member at the bedside, you know the thing that mattered most was a person who was not afraid to be there. That, in the end, is what your gift buys. Not a service. A person — willing to show up, every shift, for someone they have only just met, in the hours that matter most.

Thank you for funding that person. Thank you for funding the work. Thank you for funding the quiet.

With gratitude,

— Executive Director
