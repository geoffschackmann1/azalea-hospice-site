2Visit cadence — by priority

  • A accounts (top 20 by volume potential). Weekly touch — alternating between in-person visit, in-service education, joint patient round, and a value-add (coffee, lunch for the unit, CE-credit lunch-and-learn).
  • B accounts (the next 30). Bi-weekly in-person plus monthly value-add.
  • C accounts (long tail of 50+). Monthly drop-by, quarterly in-service. Move to A or B when volume justifies.

Liaisons own a CRM record per account: contact list, last touch, last referral, last objection, next step, next touch. We grade accounts quarterly and re-tier.

4The message — what we say

Our liaisons do not pitch. They show up, ask after specific patients, share outcomes, and offer education. The "leave-behind" story has three beats:

  • Speed. First visit within 24 hours. Symptom-crisis visit same day. We don't make people wait when they've finally said yes.
  • Continuity. The same nurse, the same aide, week after week — not a different face every visit. This matters more to families than anything else.
  • Coming back. If a patient stabilizes and graduates, we transition them and stay in touch — they often need us again.

The three things we never say: bad words about competitors, anything that could be construed as inducement (gift cards, cash, vacations, "thank-you" dinners over $25), or any clinical claim we can't put in writing.

5In-service / CE catalog

Each in-service is built to leave the receiving team smarter. Liaison schedules; clinical staff delivers.

  • "Hospice eligibility — beyond the surprise question" (45 min, 0.75 CE)
  • "Dementia at FAST 7 — when comfort is the kindest treatment" (45 min, 0.75 CE)
  • "Symptom crisis — what your hospice nurse does at 2 AM" (30 min)
  • "The conversation: how to introduce hospice without losing the family" (45 min)
  • "Veteran-specific care & the We Honor Veterans program" (30 min)
  • "What a SNF gains when a resident elects hospice" (30 min, SNF-targeted)

Goal: 24 in-services delivered FY26 across the territory. Track attendance, sign-in sheets, and follow-up referrals at 30/60/90 days.

6Compliance — the bright lines

  • Anti-Kickback Statute. No remuneration of any kind to referral sources in exchange for referrals. No exceptions.
  • Stark. Same — no financial relationships with referring physicians outside an enumerated safe harbor (rented space, etc.) all of which require legal pre-clearance.
  • Meal limits. Per OIG guidance, modest in-office meals during a bona fide educational event are permitted; we cap at $25/head, document the educational content, and obtain sign-in sheets.
  • Gifts. No gift cards. No tickets. No alcohol. Branded items of nominal value (under $10) only.
  • Inducement to patients. No waiving copays except via documented financial-hardship policy. No "free" services tied to election.

Every liaison signs an acknowledgment of the AKS / Stark training annually. Compliance officer rides along on one full liaison day per year per liaison.

7Metrics & cadence

  • Weekly: account touches per liaison (target ≥ 25/week), in-services delivered, referrals from new accounts.
  • Monthly: tier-A account health (referrals trailing 90 days vs. 90 prior), pipeline freshness, top 5 risks.
  • Quarterly: re-tier, refresh territory map, refresh key contacts after reorg/turnover.
  • Annual: full plan refresh.
