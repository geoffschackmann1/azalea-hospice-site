Inside · what hospice is

It is mostly time, attention, and quiet.

Hospice care is for people with a life-limiting illness when the goal of care has shifted from cure to comfort. It is not a place. It is a set of services that come to wherever home is — your house, an assisted-living apartment, a nursing facility, or a hospice inpatient unit when symptoms need close supervision.

Who comes

  • A nurse — usually two or three visits a week, more when needed.
  • A hospice aide — for bathing and personal care.
  • A social worker — for the family, the practical, the unspoken.
  • A chaplain — of any faith, or none. Many families ask for one.
  • A volunteer — companionship, errands, respite for the caregiver.
  • A doctor — overseeing the plan; available to your physician.
A nurse is on call 24 hours a day. Call us before you think you need to.
Inside · what it covers

Covered by Medicare, Medicaid, most insurance

  • All visits from the hospice team.
  • Medications related to the terminal illness.
  • Medical equipment — hospital bed, oxygen, walker, wheelchair.
  • Medical supplies — wound care, briefs, gloves.
  • Short-term inpatient care for symptoms not controlled at home.
  • Up to 5 days of inpatient respite so family caregivers can rest.
  • 13 months of bereavement care for the family — six months beyond what Medicare requires.

What stays the same

You keep your own physician. You keep your home. Pets are welcome. Family runs the house.

What can change

If you decide hospice is no longer right for you, you can stop at any time. Hospice is a choice — never a sentence.

Inside · when to call

Signs it may be time to ask

  • Two or more hospital stays or ER visits in the last six months.
  • Increasing fatigue; spending more time in bed than out.
  • Weight loss, less appetite, harder swallowing.
  • Treatments are causing more burden than benefit.
  • The doctor has said the words "if things don't change."
  • The family is exhausted and has nowhere to turn for help.
If you'd like a conversation with a nurse — no obligation, no pressure — we can come to your home or take a call. Most families say they wished they'd called sooner.

Outside · misconceptions

Things people believe that aren't true

"Hospice means giving up." No. It means choosing comfort and time at home over treatment that has stopped working. Some patients improve on hospice and graduate.

"It's only for the last few days." Hospice is for the last six months — and most families wish they had started earlier.

"It's a place I have to go." 95% of hospice care happens at home.

"It costs a lot." Medicare and Medicaid pay 100%. Most private insurance pays 100%. We never turn anyone away because of money.

"They take away my doctor." Your doctor stays your doctor.

"They give morphine to hasten death." Hospice doesn't hasten anything. We treat symptoms — pain, breathlessness — at the smallest dose that works.
