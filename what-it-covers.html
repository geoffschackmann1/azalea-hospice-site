What hospice covers

The Medicare hospice benefit is one of the most complete benefits in American healthcare. Most people pay $0.

When you choose hospice, the Medicare hospice benefit covers nearly everything related to the terminal illness. No copays. No deductibles. No surprise bills.

Most people pay exactly nothing. The hospice benefit is designed so that cost is never a barrier to comfort. Medicare Part A, Medicaid, and most private insurance plans cover all services listed below at 100%.

What is covered

Here is everything the hospice benefit includes:

Nursing care

A registered nurse visits regularly and is on call 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. When you call, a real nurse answers.

Doctor services

Oversight from our medical director, working alongside your own doctor. Both stay involved in your care.

Medical equipment

Hospital bed, wheelchair, oxygen, walker, commode — anything related to the hospice diagnosis. Delivered and set up at no cost.

Medical supplies

Bandages, catheters, gloves, adult briefs, and other supplies related to the illness. Delivered to your home.

Medications

All medications for symptom control and pain relief related to the terminal diagnosis. Covered in full.

Short-term inpatient care

If symptoms become difficult to manage at home, we can arrange a short stay in a hospital or facility for around-the-clock care.

Respite care

Up to 5 days in a facility to give caregivers a break. Fully covered. You can rest knowing your loved one is in good hands.

Home health aide

Help with bathing, dressing, and personal care. A certified aide comes to your home as part of the care plan.

Chaplain / spiritual counseling

Support for whatever your faith tradition is — or none at all. A chaplain is available to talk, pray, or just sit quietly.

Bereavement support

13 months of support for the family after a death — calls, letters, support groups, memorial events. Free, whether or not the death occurred on service.

What is not covered

It is important to be clear about the trade-offs. Here is what the hospice benefit does not include:

Curative treatment for the terminal illness

That is the trade-off. When you choose hospice, you stop treatments meant to cure the terminal illness and instead focus on comfort. You can still receive treatment for other conditions not related to the hospice diagnosis.

A private duty nurse

The benefit does not cover a nurse who stays in the home 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. However, a nurse is on call at all times and can come to the home when needed.

Room and board at a facility

If you live in a nursing home or assisted living, hospice covers all hospice-related care, but not the regular room and board costs. Those are paid separately.
