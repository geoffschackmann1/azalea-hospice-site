If a reporter calls.

Hospice rarely makes news for good reasons — usually it's a regulatory action, a survey deficiency, a rare patient-safety event, or a community-wide story that touches our work. This is what every staff member should know, and what only one person should do.

Owner
Executive Director
Spokesperson
ED or designee

1Who speaks

Only the Executive Director (or, if delegated in writing, the Medical Director or VP of Clinical) speaks to media on behalf of Azalea Hospice. No exceptions — including for what feels like a "soft" story (a feature on a long-tenured nurse, a community memorial). Goodwill stories still surface protected information; the routing is the same.

2If a reporter contacts you

  1. Be polite. Take their name, outlet, deadline, and what story they are working on.
  2. Do not confirm or deny anything — including whether someone is or was a patient. Even confirming a name is a HIPAA disclosure.
  3. Say, verbatim if helpful: "Thank you for reaching out. I'm not the right person to comment. Let me get our Executive Director's office your information and they will get back to you within the hour."
  4. Email the contact info to comms@azaleahospice.com immediately, and call the ED or VP of Clinical.
  5. Do not post about the inquiry on social media, Slack, or in a group text. Do not "warn" colleagues. The chain is the chain.

3If a reporter shows up in person

  • At the office: greet them at the door, do not invite them past reception, follow the steps above. They are not permitted in patient-care areas without ED approval.
  • At a patient's home: politely tell them they cannot enter; the home is private; refer them to the ED's office. Document the encounter and notify the patient's family if you have permission.
  • At the IPU: the charge nurse calls the Administrator-on-Call. Reporter is asked to wait in the lobby or leave; family privacy is absolute.
  • At a community event: a reporter is welcome to attend and report what is publicly visible. They may not interview staff on the record without ED approval. Patient identification at any community event (memorial service, etc.) is by family consent only.

4HIPAA bright lines for press

  • Patient name, condition, location of care — never disclosed without written authorization on a HIPAA-compliant release form, signed by the patient or legal representative.
  • This is true even after death. PHI is protected for 50 years post-mortem.
  • "Confirming you saw them on our admit list" is a HIPAA disclosure. So is "we can neither confirm nor deny" if the question itself includes identifying details. Decline to engage with the specifics.
  • If a family approaches us wanting to share their story publicly, route through the Director of Brand. We always confirm consent in writing, separate consents for quote and photo, and we read the final piece before it runs whenever the outlet permits.

5If the story is critical of us

If the inquiry concerns a complaint, a regulatory matter, an alleged adverse event, or litigation:

  1. The ED notifies counsel and the Board chair within two hours.
  2. We do not respond to the reporter without legal review, which we will do quickly. We do not "no comment" — we provide a brief, factual statement that respects HIPAA and the integrity of any investigation.
  3. Sample holding statement: "Azalea Hospice cares deeply about every patient and family we serve. We cannot comment on individual care due to federal privacy law, but we take all concerns seriously and review every one. We are cooperating fully with the appropriate authorities."
  4. We do not engage online — no replies on social, no comments on articles. Communication is on the record, on letterhead, through the spokesperson.
  5. Internal staff communication is calm, factual, and the same as the external statement. Rumor and speculation make a hard situation harder.

6Proactive press

  • Annual report; community memorial service; new program launches (pediatric, second IPU); volunteer recognition; "We Honor Veterans" milestones; meaningful clinical milestones (e.g., 10,000th patient).
  • Pitched by Director of Brand; reviewed by ED; never tied to fundraising appeal in the same beat.
  • Always offer a real story with consent, not a press release alone.

"What you say to a reporter goes on the record. What you say to a colleague goes everywhere. Be careful with both, and in a hard week, be careful with the second one too."
