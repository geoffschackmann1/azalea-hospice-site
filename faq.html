Hospice FAQ

Honest answers to the questions families ask most.

We know you have questions. Below are the ones we hear most often. If you don't see yours here, call us — we're happy to talk through your situation.

Hospice is fully covered by Medicare, Medicaid, and most private insurance. There are no copays, deductibles, or bills for hospice services.
Choosing hospice is not giving up. It is choosing comfort, dignity, and quality of life when a cure is no longer possible. Many families say hospice helped them make the most of the time they had together.
Yes. Most hospice care is delivered wherever you live — at home, in an assisted living facility, or in a nursing home. Our team comes to you.
Yes. You can stop hospice at any time, for any reason, and go back to curative treatment. Hospice is a choice, not a commitment.
Hospice is covered by Medicare Part A, Medicaid, and most private insurance. If you have questions about coverage, call us — we'll help you understand your options.
Yes. Your primary doctor stays involved in your care. Our hospice medical director works alongside them.
A nurse is on call 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. When you call, a real person — not an answering service — answers and can help.
Our team works with you to find a solution. Options include volunteer support, respite care at a facility, or coordinating with community resources.
No. Hospice serves people with any life-limiting illness — heart disease, lung disease, dementia, ALS, kidney failure, stroke, and many others.
Hospice is designed for people with a life expectancy of six months or less, but many people receive care for several months. The earlier you start, the more benefit you receive.
Yes. We work with skilled nursing facilities to provide hospice care wherever you call home.
Hospice supports the entire family. We provide caregiver training, emotional support, chaplain visits, and bereavement care for 13 months after a death — at no cost.
Yes. We deliver and set up any equipment related to the hospice diagnosis — hospital beds, oxygen, walkers, commodes, and more. All covered by the hospice benefit.
Palliative care is available at any stage of a serious illness, even while continuing curative treatment. Hospice is specifically for people who have decided to stop curative treatment and focus on comfort. Both focus on quality of life.
