When is hospice the right call?

A pocket reference. The federal benefit asks one question — would you be surprised if this patient died in the next six months? — and provides disease-specific guidelines for when the answer is "no."

The surprise question.

"Would I be surprised if this patient died in the next 12 months?" If the answer is no, hospice is on the table. If the answer is no for the next 6 months, hospice is appropriate today.

1By disease — clinical indicators

Cancer

  • Metastatic or stage IV with declining performance status
  • PPS ≤ 70 with continued decline
  • Refractory to or declining further disease-directed therapy
  • Recurrent infections, hospitalizations, weight loss

End-stage heart disease

  • NYHA class IV; symptomatic at rest
  • EF ≤ 20% (when measured) and optimally treated
  • Recurrent HF admissions or refractory ventricular arrhythmias
  • Persistent symptoms despite max therapy

End-stage lung disease

  • Disabling dyspnea at rest, poor response to bronchodilators
  • FEV₁ < 30% (when measured)
  • Cor pulmonale, hypoxemia on room air, hypercapnia
  • Frequent ED visits or admissions for pulmonary exacerbations

Dementia (Alzheimer's, related)

  • FAST stage 7c or beyond — non-ambulatory, non-verbal, dependent
  • One or more co-morbid event in past 12 mo: aspiration pneumonia, UTI, sepsis, stage 3+ pressure ulcer, decubitus ulcer
  • ≥ 10% weight loss in 6 mo, or albumin < 2.5 g/dL
  • Inability to maintain intake

End-stage liver disease

  • INR > 1.5, albumin < 2.5
  • One of: ascites refractory to treatment, SBP, hepatorenal syndrome, hepatic encephalopathy, recurrent variceal bleed
  • Not a transplant candidate

End-stage renal disease

  • CrCl < 10 (≤ 15 in diabetics) and not pursuing dialysis
  • Or stopping dialysis with progression
  • Symptomatic uremia, oliguria, intractable fluid overload, hyperkalemia

Stroke / coma

  • Karnofsky ≤ 40 with continued decline
  • Inability to maintain intake; aspiration pneumonia
  • Coma with no improvement after 3 days

Decline without a single defining diagnosis

  • ≥ 10% weight loss / 6 mo, or BMI < 22
  • ≥ 2 hospitalizations or ED visits in past 6 mo
  • Increased dependence in ADLs; new incontinence
  • Recurrent infections; albumin < 2.5
